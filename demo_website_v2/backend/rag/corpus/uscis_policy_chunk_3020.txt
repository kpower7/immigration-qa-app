To establish continuous physical presence during the requisite time period, T-1 principal nonimmigrant
applicants for adjustment of status must submit:
An af fidavit stating that they have been continuously physically present in the United States for
the requisite period;[9]
Copies of all pages of all the applicant’ s passports or equivalent travel document valid during the
required period (or a valid[10] explanation of why the applicant does not have a passport);[11]
A list of any departures from the United States or other evidence of such departures, (see below
for necessary details regarding departures);[12] and
Evidence of continuous physical presence in the United States.[13]
Evidence of Departures from the United States
If an applicant has departed the United States, they must submit documentation of any departure
from, and return to, the United States, while in T-1 nonimmigrant status. Such documentation must
show:
Date of departure;
Place of departure;
Length of departure;
Manner of departure (such as plane or boat);
Date of return; and 
Place of return.
Specific T ypes of Evidence to Demonstrate Continuous Physical Presence
Evidence of continuous physical presence may include:
Evidence the applicant worked in the United States, such as pay stubs or other employment
records;
Evidence that the applicant filed federal or state income tax returns;
Evidence that the applicant or their children[14] attended school in the United States, including
educational records (such as transcripts, report cards, letters) from a United States institution;
Hospital or medical records;
Documents showing installment periods, such as a series of monthly rent receipts or utility bills;
Money orders, deeds, contracts, automobile license receipts, vehicle registrations, mortgage
payments, or similar evidence;
Officially registered and issued birth certificates of children born in the United States; and
Documentation issued by any governmental or nongovernmental authority that contains the
signature, seal, or other authenticating instrument of the authorized representative of the issuing
authority if the document would normally contain such authentication.[15]8/13/25, 3:55 AM Policy Manual | USCIS
