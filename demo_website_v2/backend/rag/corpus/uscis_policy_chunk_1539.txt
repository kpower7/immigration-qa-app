Intercountry Adoption Universal Accreditation Act of 2012 (UAA),[5] which requires that adoption
service providers (ASPs) providing adoption services in orphan cases follow the same
accreditation or approval process required of ASPs providing such services in Hague Adoption
Convention cases; and
Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2014 (CAA),[6] which changed the Immigration and
Nationality Act (INA) definition of an orphan so that only one adoptive parent in a married couple
has to personally see and observe the orphan before or during the adoption proceedings in order
for the adoption to be considered “full and final” for immigration purposes.[7]
