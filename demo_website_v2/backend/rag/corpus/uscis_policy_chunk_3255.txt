4. Evidence, V alidity , USCIS Discretion, and Burden of Proof
Form I-693 and the Health-Related Grounds of Inadmissibility
In general, aliens applying for immigration benefits who are required to demonstrate that they are
admissible, such as adjustment of status applicants, must submit a Form I-693  to show they are free
from any conditions that would render them inadmissible under the health-related grounds.[20] In most
cases, the only acceptable edition of Form I-693 is the edition in use at the time the civil surgeon signs
the Form I-693. Prior editions of Form I-693 are generally not acceptable because they may lack
required information.[21]
Validity of Form I-693 Signed by a Civil Surgeon On or After November 1, 2023  
Technological advances since November 1, 2023 have enabled civil surgeons to share certain medical
data from the Form I-693  directly with CDC electronically[22] and have improved both collaboration
between CDC and USCIS and reporting of public health information collected on Form I-693 by civil
surgeons to local U.S. health departments. USCIS has determined that, in general, a properly
completed Form I-693 that is signed by a civil surgeon[23] on or after November 1, 2023 can only be
used as evidence to show that the applicant is not inadmissible under the health-related grounds for
the entire period the immigration benefit application with which the Form I-693 is submitted is pending.
Limiting the validity of the Form I-693 to the instant immigrant benefit application or request ensures
that the immigration medical examination and vaccination results are current and do not reflect
outdated findings. 
If the immigration benefit application with which the Form I-693 is submitted is withdrawn or denied,
then an applicant needs to submit a newly completed and signed Form I-693 with any future
immigration benefit application that requires an alien to demonstrate that he or she is not inadmissible
on health-related grounds.
Validity of Form I-693 Signed by a Civil Surgeon Before November 1, 2023
Before November 1, 2023, civil surgeons were not required to share or report certain information to
CDC electronically . Therefore, USCIS, in consultation with CDC, determined that a properly completed
Form I-693  signed by a civil surgeon before November 1, 2023, retains evidentiary value for 2 years
from the date of the civil surgeon’ s signature.[24]
USCIS Discretion on Form I-693 V alidity

Form I-693 or that the Form I-693 does not accurately reflect the applicant’ s medical condition such
that the applicant could be inadmissible under health-related grounds, then the of ficer has the
