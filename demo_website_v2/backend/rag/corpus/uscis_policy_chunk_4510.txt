Part L  - Revocation of Naturalization
Chapter 1- Purpose and Background
A. Purpose
Revocation of naturalization is sometimes referred to as “denaturalization.” Unlike most other
immigration proceedings that USCIS handles in an administrative setting, revocation of naturalization
can only occur in federal court.
A person’ s naturalization can be revoked either by civil proceeding or pursuant to a criminal conviction.
For civil revocation of naturalization, the United States Attorney’ s Office must file the revocation of
naturalization actions in Federal District Court.[1] For criminal revocation of naturalization, the U.S.
Attorney’ s Office files criminal charges in Federal District Court.[2]
