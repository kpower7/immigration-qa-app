4. U.S. Federal Government W orkers
The same policies and procedures that apply to military members also apply to non-military U.S.
federal government workers currently working abroad on of ficial government orders. Evidence of
government employment or orders may include:
A copy of travel orders issued by the government;
A copy of government identification (such as a PIV card or credential);
Copies of pay stubs or W2s issued by the government;
Copies of bank account statements showing pay from a U.S. government entity; and
An APO, FPO, or DFO mailing address.
E. CPRs with Pending Naturalization Applications
Because of dif ferences in processing times between the Form I-751  and the Application for
Naturalization ( Form N-400 ) and because CPRs are eligible to apply for naturalization (if otherwise
eligible),[38] there may be instances when a CPR applies for naturalization while the CPR’ s Form I-
751 is still pending. If the CPR has a pending Form N-400, USCIS adjudicates the Form I-751 before
or at the same time as the Form N-400.
In most cases, CPRs must have an approved Form I-751 before USCIS may naturalize them.
However , there are limited circumstances in which the CPR is not required to file a Form I-751 before
naturalizing.[39]
