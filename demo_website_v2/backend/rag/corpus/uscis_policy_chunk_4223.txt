Part H - Children of U.S. Citizens
Chapter 1 - Purpose and Background
A. Purpose
United States laws allow for children to acquire U.S. citizenship other than through birth in the United
States.[1] Persons who were born outside of the United States to a U.S. citizen parent or parents may
acquire or derive U.S. citizenship at birth. Persons, including adopted children,[2] may also acquire
citizenship after birth, but before the age of 18, through their U.S. citizen parents.
Previously , acquisition of citizenship generally related to those persons who became U.S. citizens at
the time of birth, and derivation of citizenship to those who became U.S. citizens after birth due to the
naturalization of a parent.
In general, current nationality laws only refer to acquisition of citizenship for persons who automatically
become U.S. citizens either at the time of birth or after . In general, a person must meet the applicable
